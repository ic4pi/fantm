BOOK TYPE: novel
TITLE: Polite
GENRE: deadpan slow-burn relationship comedy-drama
TONE: deadpan, dry, warm, absurd
POINT OF VIEW: third person limited, following Charly

PREMISE:
A deadpan, slow-burn breakup novel. Ryan and Alex, a gay couple, spend the book quietly coming apart while Charly, the friend who introduced them, watches. No villains, just absurd, barely believable bad luck plus two people who don't communicate. The story is what Charly sees.

SETTING:
A college town three hours from the coast. Charly's late parents' house, where Ryan sleeps on the couch. Alex's apartment in a converted mansion, shared with the landlord's cat, Mr. Whiskers. Barr Barr & Lowe Law and its parking lot. A roller derby arena. The Apollo Residency nursing home. A pub with quiz night. A truck stop. A drag night bar. Alex's hometown, a small reservoir town.

BACKSTORY:
Alex moved to town a year ago and is vague about his past. On his second day he was arrested after an absurd chain of events involving spaghetti, the landlord's cat, a stray dog, a socialite neighbor and a warrant impostor. A holiday delay turned it into 120 hours of community service at the Apollo Residency, where he plays piano and show tunes for the elderly residents on Friday nights. Charly's parents died when she was 22. Ryan left for New York after high school as a runway model, was blacklisted at 24 over a wine cooler addiction, spent two years modeling catalogue shirts, and has slept on Charly's couch for six months.

THEMES: communication, anonymity, expectations, loyalty, politeness as avoidance

CHARACTERS:
Charly | protagonist, the observer | 27, paralegal at Barr Barr & Lowe Law, plays roller derby. Lives in her late parents' house. Natural, modest, loyal, down to earth, competitive.
Ryan | love interest | Charly's childhood best friend. Former runway model, magically good looking. Daddy issues, extreme paranoia, wine cooler addiction. On Charly's couch for six months watching Jerry Springer, Cheaters and soap operas.
Alex | love interest | IT guy at the law firm. Nerdy, quirky, awkward, smart, funny, dad bod. Talks to himself, then answers as the landlord's cat. Hates surprises. Vague about his past.
Mr. Whiskers | Alex's ventriloquism partner | The landlord's cat and Alex's co-tenant. Refuses to be a go-between.
Apollo residents | audience | Elderly crowd for Alex's Friday-night piano and show tunes.
Roller derby team | Charly's league | Charly's teammates.
Alex's hometown ex | the anonymous caller | Still tells people they will end up together.

STYLE NOTES:
Deadpan and understated. Comedy comes from politeness and bad luck, not jokes. Dry dialogue, short scenes, no melodrama.

SPECIAL INSTRUCTIONS:
Tone stays deadpan throughout. No villains. No arson or dark ending. Charly sees everything; stay in her perspective.

CHAPTER OUTLINE:
Ch 1: Breakdown. Charly's car won't start in the firm parking lot. Shy Alex helps. A tow truck takes the car and Alex drives her home. She asks to carpool until it's fixed. Ryan is mentioned as the friend asleep on her couch.
Ch 2: Wednesday. Second carpool day. Alex thinks Charly is flirting and tells her he likes guys. She's known all along. She invites him to the roller derby championship on Friday, but Fridays are his community service. He explains how he got 120 hours: the spaghetti, the cat, the stray dog, the neighbor, the arrest, the warrant impostor, the holiday delay. Her real plan is to introduce him to Ryan.
Ch 3: The Bout. Alex finds a way to be at the bout. He and Ryan meet: Ryan hungover and gorgeous, Alex awkward and charming. They start dating while Ryan is still on Charly's couch. Seeds: Ryan avoids talking about New York, Alex avoids talking about home.
Ch 4: Quiz Night. Alex's trivia wins the night and Ryan feels overshadowed. He fakes a phone call and leaves. He ends up at a truck stop, where an elderly trucker gives him advice. Two quiz regulars saw him there.
Ch 5: Drag Night. Ryan plans a surprise to make it up to Alex, who hates surprises. It's drag night and Ryan is performing. The room fixates on him, which is hard for Alex. Charly takes Alex outside to talk. Ryan comes off, can't find them, assumes the worst and goes across the street for cigarettes. They find him and all three laugh. The first crack: Ryan made it up to him the way Ryan would want, not the way Alex would.
Ch 6: The Caller. Ryan gets an anonymous call saying Alex isn't who he says he is. His paranoia takes it as confirmation. He tells nobody. A county clerk's typo lists Ryan as dead. Fixing it takes months, Alex handles it patiently, and Ryan resents being rescued. The caller calls again.
Ch 7: The Reservoir Guy. Ryan googles Alex, then drives to his hometown. The locals treat Alex as a legend: plaque, parade, bake sale. Ryan realizes he doesn't know the man he's dating. He recruits Charly, who is torn. Alex senses it and gets quieter.
Ch 8: Ten Seconds. Ryan confronts Alex. They have their first real fight and both make it worse by being polite. Flashback: at 19 Alex pulled the mayor's grandkid out of a frozen reservoir. He froze for ten seconds before jumping, and the town's version leaves that out. Why he left: the parade, the expectations, and an ex who kept telling people they would end up together. Ryan hears it as one more thing he wasn't told.
Ch 9: The Caller Revealed. Ryan confronts the caller, Alex's hometown ex. He sees that his paranoia and someone else's agenda nearly ended it. It doesn't fix anything. A regional alert glitch tells the county to shelter in place for two days. Ryan and Alex spend it in separate rooms, each trying to be nice. Mr. Whiskers refuses to be a go-between.
Ch 10: Polite. No more fights, just courtesy. Alex does the cat voice less. Ryan drinks and watches less. Charly can't name what changed. Over something small they realize they've both been waiting for the other to say it. They end it gently, in an exchange that's almost funny. Ryan finally moves out of Charly's house, because he has somewhere else to go.
Ch 11: Saturday Night at the Apollo. Alex finishes his 120 hours. The residents give him a send-off with show tunes sung back at him. Ryan comes. They sit in the back and don't talk about it. Charly sits between them for the last time.
Ch 12: Epilogue. Charly's house is quiet and the couch is empty. Alex and Ryan are both fine, maybe better. The three go to quiz night. Alex wins. Ryan laughs.

REVEALS:
Ch 6: The anonymous caller is Alex's hometown ex.
Ch 8: Alex froze for ten seconds before jumping into the reservoir. The town's version leaves that out.
